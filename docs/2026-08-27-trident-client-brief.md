## 1. What we are looking for, in plain language

We are hunting one specific shape on a **30-minute candlestick chart**, and only during the
**first three and a half hours** of the trading day.

The idea behind it, stated simply:

> When a stock jumps up very fast, it leaves a "gap" — a price range that barely traded.
> Often price drifts back to that gap later. **If buyers defend the middle of that gap
> aggressively, that is the signal.** We enter just as they defend it, with a stop-loss just
> below — so the risk is small and clearly defined.

The whole appeal is *where the stop sits*. Because entry is right on top of the level that
would prove us wrong, the risk per trade is very small — often under half a percent.

## 2. The pattern, step by step

Check these **in order**. If any step fails, stop — there is no trade.

```
   Step 1: THE GAP                    Step 2: THE RETURN

        |                                  |
       ###  <- candle 3                   ###
       ###                                ###
    .........  gap top                 .........
    :       :                          :   |   :  <- price comes back
    : GAP   :   <- untraded space      :  ###  :     into the gap
    :       :                          :   |   :
    .........  gap bottom              .........
       ###
       ###  <- candle 1
        |
                                       Step 3: THE DOJI
   candle 3's LOW is ABOVE                   |
   candle 1's HIGH                          ###   <- small body
                                             |    <- long lower wick
                                             |       dips below the
                                                     gap middle, then
                                                     closes back above
```

### Step 1 — Find the gap (09:15 to 12:45 only)

Look for **three consecutive 30-minute candles** where the **third candle's lowest price is
above the first candle's highest price**. The space between them never traded — that is the
gap.

Write down the **middle of that gap**. Everything below depends on this one number.

> *Example:* candle 1 tops out at ₹100.20. Candle 3 bottoms at ₹101.00. The gap is
> ₹100.20–₹101.00, and its middle is **₹100.60**.

### Step 2 — Check the trend is clean

On the same 30-minute chart, the **5, 9, 13 and 21 moving averages must be stacked in order**
— the 5 on top, then 9, then 13, then 21 underneath. They should look like layers, not
spaghetti.

If they are crossing over each other, **skip it**. This is the trader's own rule and he is
firm about it.

### Step 3 — Wait for price to come back to the gap's middle

Most of the time it will not, and there is no trade. That is normal and expected.

### Step 4 — The return candle must be a **doji** — this is the critical one

A "doji" is a candle with a **small body and a long lower wick**. It means price dipped down,
sellers tried to push it lower, and buyers shoved it straight back up — all inside one
30-minute candle.

**It must:**
- dip **below** the gap's middle with its wick, and
- **close back above** the gap's middle, and
- have a **small body** — roughly, the body should be **less than a third** of the candle's
  full height.

> **A big, solid candle at that same level is NOT this setup.** It looks superficially
> similar and it is the single most common way to get this wrong. A large body means one side
> won the candle outright. We want the *fight and rejection*, which is what the small body
> plus long wick shows.

### Step 5 — The next candle must close **below** the doji's high

One more 30-minute candle. It must finish **below the highest point of the doji**.

If it closes above, **the setup is dead** — even though it may look strong. The reason is
practical: if price runs up too far, our entry ends up far away from our stop-loss, and the
small-risk logic that makes the whole thing worthwhile disappears.

### If all five pass

- **Entry:** the closing price of that fifth candle
- **Stop-loss:** just below the **low of the doji** (step 4's candle)
- **Target:** four times the distance from entry to stop

**The stop-loss is never moved further away to make a trade "fit".** If the stop feels too
tight, the correct response is to skip the trade, not to widen it.

Whatever appears tomorrow is **recorded on paper, not traded.** We are building a forward

Realistically this needs **months, not days**. At roughly one setup every three sessions

## 6. What we honestly do not know

- **Whether this makes money.** Every version we tested lost, though the sample of 22 trades
  was too small to be conclusive.
- **Whether the timing window transfers** from currency markets to Indian equities.
- **Whether the "strong close" indicator matches** the one the original trader uses — he
  names a tool but never explains its formula, so ours is a reconstruction.
- **What happens in a different market.** All our data comes from a single ~3-month stretch,
  which is the maximum history available to us at 30-minute resolution.

One measured fact worth knowing, because it works against this strategy: because the
stop-loss is placed so close, **brokerage and taxes consume an unusually large share of the
risk** — on average about half of the amount risked per trade, and on the tightest examples
more than the entire amount risked. Any assessment has to clear that hurdle before anything
is left over.
